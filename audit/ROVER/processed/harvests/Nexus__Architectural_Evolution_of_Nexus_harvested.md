## 1. AI OS Architecture — Persistent Architectural Pressures Converging on Identity, Memory, and Governance
**Status:** `Agreed`

### Architectural Intent
Recognize that multiple independent builders are converging on the same AI OS architecture due to persistent architectural pressures: identity (how agents exist as continuous processes rather than stateless invocations), memory (how knowledge persists across sessions), and governance (how actions are constrained and audited). Nexus is not an 'agent framework' — it is an emergent AI operating system where event loops are essential for persistent cognition and agency. The architecture solves for identity being the critical engineering problem, not intelligence, when agents operate as continuous processes.

### Requirements & Acceptance Criteria
- [ ] Event loops required for persistent cognition — stateless model calls are insufficient
- [ ] Identity, not intelligence, is the critical engineering problem for continuous agents
- [ ] Governance must be embedded in the substrate, not layered on top
- [ ] Memory persistence across sessions is a first-class architectural concern

## 2. Institutional Engineering — Designing Institutions for AI, Not Applications
**Status:** `Agreed`

### Architectural Intent
Shift from traditional software development to institutional engineering: designing systems that manage interactions, persistent roles, and organizational governance (rules, legitimacy, coordination) rather than single-purpose applications. The system becomes an institution that coordinates AI agents through structured roles with defined authorities, jurisdictions, and obligations. This moves beyond 'agent orchestration' into designing the constitutional fabric of an AI-governed organization.

### Requirements & Acceptance Criteria
- [ ] Institutions, not applications — persistent role structures with governance
- [ ] Roles have defined: authorities, jurisdictions, and obligations
- [ ] Legitimacy and coordination are first-class system concerns
- [ ] Constitutional fabric — rules that govern how rules are made and enforced

## 3. Metacognition and Self-Governance — AI as Process Supervisor via Constitution-Driven Reflection
**Status:** `Agreed`

### Architectural Intent
Enable AI agents to function as process supervisors through metacognition and self-governance, as demonstrated by the Qwen+constitution experiment. The AI performs meta-reasoning, reflection, and self-generated work by operating under a constitution — a set of governing principles that the AI applies to its own reasoning. This enables the AI to supervise its own processes, generate its own work items, and reflect on its own outputs without external prompting. Constitution-driven reflection replaces external orchestration with internalized governance.

### Requirements & Acceptance Criteria
- [ ] Constitution: governing principles that AI applies to its own reasoning
- [ ] Meta-reasoning: AI reflects on its own thought processes
- [ ] Self-generated work: AI produces new work items autonomously
- [ ] Process supervision: AI monitors and adjusts its own execution
- [ ] Constitution must be readable and auditable by humans
