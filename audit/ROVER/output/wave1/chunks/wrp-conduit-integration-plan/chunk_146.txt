Here’s the next companion doc as the operational layer for the review-state schema.docs/nebula/review-workflow.mdMarkdown# Nebula Review Workflow**Status:** Draft**Owner:** Nebula governance / corpus maintenance**Purpose:** Define how review state is assigned, updated, interpreted, and used to prioritize attention across Nebula artifacts, sections, and chunks.---# 1. PurposeNebula stores a growing corpus of architecture notes, specifications, migration plans, ontology documents, implementation plans, and execution-adjacent material. Not all corpus material is equally current, equally trustworthy, or equally relevant to active work.This document defines a **review workflow overlay** that allows Nebula users and roles to mark corpus material as:- needing review- under active review- reviewed- current focus- outdated- questioned- supersededThe review workflow exists to answer a different question than lifecycle status.## Lifecycle status answers:- Is this artifact active, draft, deprecated, or superseded in the corpus?## Review status answers:- What should we pay attention to right now?- What should be trusted by default?- What needs inspection before it influences implementation?- What should be deprioritized or excluded from normal retrieval?Review state is therefore a **triage and trust overlay** on top of the corpus, not a replacement for artifact lifecycle semantics.---# 2. Core Principles## 2.1 Review state is separate from artifact lifecycleAn item may be:- `status = ACTIVE` and `reviewStatus = NEEDS_REVIEW`- `status = ACTIVE` and `reviewStatus = CURRENT_FOCUS`- `status = SUPERSEDED` and `reviewStatus = SUPERSEDED_CONFIRMED`- `status = DRAFT` and `reviewStatus = UNDER_REVIEW`Lifecycle describes what the artifact **is**.Review state describes what the team should **do about it**.## 2.2 Review state is primarily an attention and trust signalReview state is used to:- focus corpus maintenance- guide retrieval ranking- reduce accidental reliance on stale documents- identify blocking or suspect specs- surface active areas of architecture work## 2.3 Artifact-level review is the default starting pointNebula SHOULD support review state at:- artifact level- section level- chunk levelHowever, the initial operational workflow assumes:- **artifact-level review is primary**- section/chunk review is optional and targetedThe system MUST NOT require chunk-by-chunk review to be useful.## 2.4 Review state is allowed to be provisionalReview state is not a certification regime. It is acceptable to mark an item:- `QUESTIONED`- `OUTDATED`- `NEEDS_REVIEW`without yet knowing the final replacement or fix. The goal is to make uncertainty visible and actionable.---# 3. Review Scope LevelsNebula review annotations may apply at three scopes.## 3.1 Artifact scopeApplies to an entire document / artifact such as:- `wrp-v1-protocol`- `wrp-v1_1-dag`- `wrp-v1_2-traversal`- `wrp-migration-plan`Artifact scope is the default and most common review target.## 3.2 Section scopeApplies to a specific section within an artifact when the document is mixed-quality.Example:- the WRP protocol document is generally current- but the `Execution Semantics / Receipts` section is outdatedSection review SHOULD be used when:- only one part of a large artifact is stale or questionable- the artifact should remain broadly retrievable without blanket demotion## 3.3 Chunk scopeApplies to a specific chunk when review needs to be precise.Chunk review SHOULD be used sparingly and mainly when:- a particular invariant is suspect- a specific migration step is obsolete- a specific rationale chunk remains valuable even inside an outdated section---# 4. Review Status VocabularyThe following review statuses are defined by `schemas/core/nebula-review-state.jsonld`.## 4.1 `UNREVIEWED`The item has not yet been reviewed for current validity or relevance.Use when:- a document was imported but not yet triaged- a new chunk has not been evaluated## 4.2 `NEEDS_REVIEW`The item should be reviewed soon because it may be stale, incomplete, or important to current work.Use when:- the document is likely relevant to current implementation- the document predates a major architectural shift- the document appears important but confidence is low## 4.3 `UNDER_REVIEW`The item is actively being reviewed, rewritten, or reconciled.Use when:- you are actively comparing it against current architecture or code- Builder/Architect/Inspector are working through it now## 4.4 `REVIEWED`The item has been reviewed and is believed to be usable in normal work.Use when:- the document is current enough for default retrieval- known issues are minor or already called out## 4.5 `CURRENT_FOCUS`The item is a current focus of architecture or implementation attention and should be surfaced prominently.Use when:- active work depends on this item now- the item is central to the current execution stream- the item is the present working center of a subsystem redesign## 4.6 `OUTDATED`The item is known to be out of date relative to the current architecture, runtime, or workflow.Use when:- the document predates a material redesign- its prescriptions are no longer safe defaults- it still has historical value but should be demoted## 4.7 `QUESTIONED`The item contains claims, assumptions, or structure whose correctness is in doubt.Use when:- parts of the document conflict with newer understanding- the model may be wrong, not merely old- the document should be examined before it is trusted## 4.8 `SUPERSEDED_CONFIRMED`The item has been superseded by a newer artifact or chunk and should normally be excluded from default retrieval.Use when:- there is a clear replacement artifact or replacement chunk set- the supersession is intentional and not merely suspected---# 5. Review PriorityReview priority answers: **how urgently should this item be revisited?**Allowed values:- `CRITICAL`- `HIGH`- `MEDIUM`- `LOW`## 5.1 Priority guidance### `CRITICAL`Use when:- the item is blocking implementation- the item is actively misleading current work- the item governs current execution and is likely wrong or stale### `HIGH`Use when:- the item is central to near-term architecture or implementation- review delay would materially slow work or degrade decisions### `MEDIUM`Use when:- the item matters, but not immediately- it should be triaged in the near future### `LOW`Use when:- review is backlog hygiene- the item is historical or peripheral---# 6. Attention ReasonsAttention reasons explain **why** an item needs review or focus.The first review-state schema defines these reasons:- `BLOCKING_IMPLEMENTATION`- `OUT_OF_DATE_WITH_RUNTIME`- `OUT_OF_DATE_WITH_ARCHITECTURE`- `CONFLICTS_WITH_NEW_MODEL`- `REQUIRES_MIGRATION`- `DUPLICATED_ELSEWHERE`- `NEEDS_LEVEL_REVIEW`- `NEEDS_CHUNKING_REVIEW`Multiple reasons MAY be attached to the same item.## ExampleA migration document might be:- `reviewStatus = NEEDS_REVIEW`- `reviewPriority = HIGH`- `attentionReason = [REQUIRES_MIGRATION, OUT_OF_DATE_WITH_ARCHITECTURE]`---# 7. Who May Set Review State## 7.1 Human authority is primaryThe human operator is the authoritative source of review state.In practice, this means you may set review state manually based on reading, comparison, or implementation experience.## 7.2 Roles may propose review state, but not silently finalize itThe following roles may recommend review changes:- **Inspector**- flags contradictions, stale invariants, missing migration updates, or retrieval hazards- **Architect**- flags architectural obsolescence, boundary drift, or rationale conflicts- **Builder**- flags implementation-blocking ambiguity or mismatch with actual code- **Explorer**- flags duplicate or related documents that may need consolidationThese role recommendations SHOULD be recorded as annotations or proposals, but SHOULD NOT silently overwrite the effective review state unless explicitly authorized.## 7.3 Importers may initialize, not decideImporters and projectors MAY set initial values such as:- `UNREVIEWED`- `NEEDS_REVIEW` when the source predates a known migration boundary- `OUTDATED` when explicit source metadata says soHowever, importers SHOULD NOT autonomously mark an item `REVIEWED` or `SUPERSEDED_CONFIRMED`.---# 8. Effective Review State ModelNebula may store multiple review annotations over time. The system therefore needs a rule for determining the **effective review state** used in retrieval and dashboards.## 8.1 Effective review state per scopeEach reviewable object (artifact, section, chunk) SHOULD expose a single **effective review state** for operational use.This effective state may be materialized directly on the object row or computed from annotations.## 8.2 Default precedence ruleIf multiple annotations exist for the same object, the effective review state SHOULD be determined by:1. most recent human-authored authoritative annotation2. otherwise most recent approved role recommendation3. otherwise importer-initialized default4. otherwise `UNREVIEWED`## 8.3 Status severity tie-breakIf multiple candidate annotations are equally authoritative and recent enough to conflict, use the following severity ordering to avoid false optimism:- `CURRENT_FOCUS`- `UNDER_REVIEW`- `QUESTIONED`- `OUTDATED`- `NEEDS_REVIEW`- `REVIEWED`- `UNREVIEWED`- `SUPERSEDED_CONFIRMED`This severity ordering is for tie-breaking only and MUST NOT be interpreted as a universal ranking of importance.## 8.4 Why `SUPERSEDED_CONFIRMED` is not treated as “most severe”Superseded material is often safe to ignore rather than urgent to fix. It is not necessarily a live problem. Therefore it should not outrank `QUESTIONED` or `CURRENT_FOCUS` in attention logic.---# 9.
