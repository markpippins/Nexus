## 1. Knowledge Engineering as Ecosystem — Bridging Data Engineering, Information Architecture, and Knowledge Modeling
**Status:** `Observed`

### Architectural Intent
Frame knowledge engineering as the creation of rich, multidisciplinary knowledge ecosystems — not IT development. The ecosystem bridges data engineering (facts and storage), information architecture (structure and navigation), and knowledge modeling (meaning and relationships). It is an end-to-end system handling capture, classification, and secure integration of internal and external knowledge across multiple workflows, products, and processes. Knowledge infrastructure is a business process in itself, not a single product.

### Requirements & Acceptance Criteria
- [ ] Three domains: data engineering, information architecture, knowledge modeling
- [ ] End-to-end: capture → classify → integrate → serve
- [ ] Crosses multiple workflows, products, and processes
- [ ] Knowledge infrastructure = business process, not product
- [ ] Human-in-the-loop verification for high-stakes decisions

## 2. Knowledge Worker vs Knowledge Engineer — Technical Builders vs Ecosystem Participants
**Status:** `Observed`

### Architectural Intent
Distinguish between knowledge engineers (technical experts who build the knowledge systems — design ontologies, implement pipelines, configure infrastructure) and knowledge workers (anyone contributing to or curating the knowledge ecosystem — domain experts entering information, reviewers validating outputs, consumers applying insights). Both roles are essential. The system must support both the technical builders who create the substrate and the domain participants who populate and validate it.

### Requirements & Acceptance Criteria
- [ ] Knowledge engineers: build ontologies, pipelines, infrastructure
- [ ] Knowledge workers: contribute, curate, validate, consume
- [ ] System must support both roles with appropriate interfaces
- [ ] Human-in-the-loop verification for high-stakes decisions
- [ ] Upskilling organizations prevents knowledge degradation
